1| In the department… but it's better not to say which department.
2| There's nothing [touchier]{сердитее} than every sort of department, regiment, [office]{канцелярий} and, in a word, every sort of [official class]{должностных сословий}.
3| Nowadays every [private]{частный} person considers the whole of society [insulted]{оскорбленным} in his own person.
4| They say that quite recently a [petition]{просьба} came in from a certain [district police chief]{капитан-исправника} of I don't remember which town, in which he [sets out]{излагает} clearly that state [ordinances]{постановления} are [perishing]{гибнут} and that his sacred name is being taken absolutely [in vain]{всуе}.
5| And [as proof]{в доказательство} he [attached]{приложил} to the petition an enormous volume of some romantic [composition]{сочинения} in which a district police chief appears every ten pages, in places even completely [drunk]{в пьяном виде}.
6| And so, [to avoid]{во избежание} any unpleasantness, we'd better call the department [in question]{о котором идет дело} "a certain department."
note: капитан-исправника | district police chief | In tsarist Russia the «капитан-исправник» headed the rural police of a district. Gogol mocks the touchiness of officials who see every story as an attack on them.
note: всуе | in vain | Church Slavonic, from the commandment «Не произноси имени Господа Бога твоего всуе». Applying it to a police chief's name is the joke.
note: о котором идет дело | in question, being discussed | «Идти о чём-то» in the sense "to be about": «речь идёт о…», «дело идёт о…».
note: преогромнейший | enormous, the most gigantic | A doubled intensifier: «пре-» plus the superlative «-ейший». Gogol uses it for mock grandeur.
q: main | Why does the narrator refuse to name the department? | *He fears that touchy officials will take offense | The department no longer exists | He has forgotten its name | It is a state secret
q: detail | What did the police chief attach to his petition? | A list of his medals | *A huge romantic novel featuring a police chief, sometimes drunk | A map of his town | A letter from the governor
q: tone | What is the tone of this opening? | Solemn and patriotic | *Mock-serious satire | Tragic | Dry and technical
q: inference | What is Gogol really mocking? | Romantic novels | *The vanity and oversensitivity of officialdom | The Church | Private citizens
sum: Before beginning the story, the narrator refuses to name the department, joking that officials take every mention as an insult to the whole service; he cites a police chief who complained that a novel mocked his office.

1| And so, in a certain department there served a certain [official]{чиновник}; an official who could not be called very [remarkable]{замечательный}: short, somewhat [pockmarked]{рябоват}, somewhat [reddish]{рыжеват}, somewhat even [weak-sighted]{подслеповат} in appearance, with a small [bald spot]{лысиной} on his forehead, [wrinkles]{морщинами} on both cheeks and a complexion of the kind called [hemorrhoidal]{геморроидальным}...
2| What can you do! The Petersburg climate is [to blame]{виноват}.
3| As for his [rank]{чина} (for with us the rank must be announced first of all), he was what is called an [eternal titular councillor]{вечный титулярный советник}, at whom, as is well known, various writers have [mocked and jeered to their heart's content]{натрунились и наострились вдоволь}, writers who have the [laudable]{похвальное} [habit]{обыкновенье} of [bearing down]{налегать} on those who cannot bite.
4| The official's surname was Bashmachkin.
5| From the name itself it's plain that it once [came from]{произошла} a [shoe]{башмака}; but when, at what time and in what way it came from a shoe, none of this is known.
6| His father, and his grandfather, and even his [brother-in-law]{шурин}, and absolutely all the Bashmachkins went about in boots, changing the [soles]{подметки} only about three times a year.
note: рябоват | somewhat pockmarked | The suffix «-оват» softens an adjective: «рябой» means pockmarked, «рябоват» rather pockmarked. Gogol stacks «рябоват, рыжеват, подслеповат» for comic effect.
note: вечный титулярный советник | eternal titular councillor | A low rank, 9th class on the Table of Ranks. «Вечный» means he was stuck there for life, a stock comic figure in 1830s–40s literature.
note: налегать на тех, которые не могут кусаться | bearing down on those who can't bite back | A dig at writers who mock the weak because it's safe. «Налегать» means to lean on hard.
note: шурин | brother-in-law (wife's brother) | Russian has separate words: «шурин» is the wife's brother, «деверь» the husband's brother, «зять» a sister's husband or a son-in-law.
q: main | What is the main purpose of this passage? | *To introduce Akaky Akakievich as an utterly unremarkable little clerk | To describe St. Petersburg's climate | To explain the Table of Ranks | To tell the history of shoemaking
q: detail | What rank does the clerk hold? | General | *Titular councillor | Collegiate assessor | Police chief
q: inference | Why does Gogol joke about the origin of the name Bashmachkin? | He wants to praise the family | *To deepen the comic, absurd smallness of the hero | Because the family were shoemakers | To criticize footwear prices
q: idiom | «Что ж делать!» here means | *"What can you do!" | "What should I do now?" | "Let's get to work!" | "Who did this?"
sum: Gogol introduces his hero: a short, pockmarked, balding, permanently low-ranking clerk named Bashmachkin, a name absurdly derived from "shoe," while mocking writers who pick on such defenseless people.

q: inference | Why does his wife happily give him bread? | She loves him dearly | *It leaves more coffee for her | The bread is stale | She wants him to go to work
q: tone | The tone is | tragic | *comic and matter-of-fact, before something absurd | frightening | lyrical
sum: On March 25th, the Petersburg barber Ivan Yakovlevich wakes to the smell of fresh bread. He asks for bread and onion instead of coffee, and his wife, pleased to have more coffee for herself, tosses him a loaf.
