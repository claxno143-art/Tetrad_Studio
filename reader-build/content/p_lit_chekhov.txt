=== id: chekhov-dama-2
title: Дама с собачкой: Гуров
author: А. П. Чехов
level: 3+
genre: literature
src: RussianNovels/corpus/Chekhov_Dama.txt
from: Ему не было еще сорока
to: он не мог бы прожить и двух дней.
---
1| He was not yet forty, but he already had a twelve-year-old daughter and two sons at [gymnasium]{сына-гимназиста}.
2| They had [married him off]{женили} early, when he was still a second-year student, and now his wife seemed [half again]{в полтора раза} as old as he was.
3| She was a tall woman with dark eyebrows, upright, [imposing]{важная}, [solid]{солидная}, and, as she called herself, a [thinking]{мыслящая} woman.
4| She read a great deal, left the [hard sign]{ъ} out of her letters, called her husband not Dmitri but Dimitri, while he [secretly]{втайне} thought her [narrow-minded]{недалекой}, limited and [inelegant]{неизящной}, was afraid of her and didn't like being at home.
5| He had begun [cheating on]{изменять} her long ago, cheated often, and probably for that reason almost always [spoke]{отзывался} [badly]{дурно} of women, and when they were discussed [in his presence]{в его присутствии} he called them:
6| "The [lower race]{низшая раса}!"
7| It seemed to him that he had been taught enough by [bitter experience]{горьким опытом} to call them whatever he liked, and yet without the "lower race" he could not have lasted two days.
note: женили | married him off | The verb is active and plural: others (his family) arranged the marriage. «Жениться» is what a man does himself; «женить» is to marry someone off.
note: не писала в письмах ъ | left the hard sign out of her letters | Before the 1918 reform, words ending in a consonant were written with a final «ъ». Dropping it was a progressive, slightly showy gesture in the 1890s.
note: мыслящая | thinking (as a self-label) | «Мыслящая женщина» was a fashionable phrase for an educated, emancipated woman. Chekhov's «как она сама себя называла» makes it ironic.
note: отзывался | spoke of, gave an opinion of | «Отзываться о ком-то» means to speak of someone in a certain way, here «дурно», badly. Compare «отзыв», a review.
note: научен горьким опытом | taught by bitter experience | A set phrase: «горький опыт» is hard-won experience.
q: main | What does this passage mainly do? | *It describes Gurov's marriage and his attitude to women | It tells how Gurov met his wife | It explains why Gurov came to Yalta | It describes Gurov's children
q: detail | How did Gurov come to be married? | He fell in love at the gymnasium | *He was married off young, as a second-year student | He married late, after forty | His wife proposed to him
q: inference | Why does Gurov call women "the lower race"? | He has studied the question scientifically | His wife taught him the phrase | *He is often unfaithful and speaks badly of women, perhaps to justify himself | He has never met interesting women
q: tone | What is the narrator's attitude toward Gurov? | Openly admiring | *Detached and quietly ironic | Furious and accusing | Sentimental and pitying
q: idiom | «Не мог бы прожить и двух дней» suggests that Gurov | *cannot do without women's company | will die in two days | plans to leave Yalta | dislikes his work
sum: Gurov, not yet forty, was married off young to a stern, self-consciously intellectual woman he fears and doesn't love. He cheats on her constantly and sneers at women as "the lower race," yet he can hardly live two days without them.

=== id: chekhov-dama-3

author: А. П. Чехов
level: 3+
genre: literature
src: RussianNovels/corpus/Chekhov_Dama.txt
from: И вот однажды под вечер он обедал в саду
to: Дама взглянула на него и тотчас же опустила глаза.
---
1| And then one day [toward evening]{под вечер} he was dining in the garden, and the lady in the beret came up [without hurrying]{не спеша} to take the [next table]{соседний стол}.
2| Her expression, her [walk]{походка}, her dress, her [hairstyle]{прическа} told him that she was from [respectable]{порядочного} society, married, in Yalta for the first time and alone, and that she was bored here…
3| There was a lot of untruth in the stories about the [loose]{нечистоте} local [morals]{нравов}; he [despised]{презирал} them and knew that such stories were mostly [made up]{сочиняются} by people who would gladly [sin]{грешили} themselves if they knew how; but when the lady sat down at the next table three steps from him, he remembered those stories of easy conquests and trips to the mountains, and the [tempting]{соблазнительная} thought of a quick, [fleeting]{мимолетной} [affair]{связи}, of a romance with an unknown woman whose name you don't even know, suddenly [took hold of]{овладела} him.
4| He [beckoned]{поманил} the spitz [affectionately]{ласково}, and when it came up, he [shook his finger]{погрозил} at it.
5| The spitz [growled]{заворчал}.
6| Gurov shook his finger again.
7| The lady glanced at him and at once [lowered her eyes]{опустила глаза}.
note: порядочного общества | respectable society | «Порядочный» means decent, respectable. «Порядочный человек» is a decent person; «порядочно» can also mean "quite a lot."
note: связи | affair, liaison | «Связь» usually means a connection or communication; with a man and a woman, especially «мимолетная связь», it means a love affair.
note: погрозил ему пальцем | shook his finger at it | «Грозить пальцем» is the playful or warning gesture of a raised wagging finger. Gurov uses the dog to start a conversation.
q: main | What happens in this passage? | *Gurov uses the dog to make first contact with the lady | The lady asks Gurov about Yalta | Gurov decides to leave the resort | The lady's husband arrives
q: detail | What does Gurov conclude from the lady's appearance? | She is a local resident | She is poor and works in Yalta | *She is respectable, married, new to Yalta, alone and bored | She is a famous actress
q: inference | What is Gurov's real intention? | To buy the spitz | *To start a brief affair | To warn the lady about local gossip | To ask for directions to the mountains
q: tone | How does the last line, the lady lowering her eyes, feel? | Hostile | *Shy, with a hint of interest | Mocking | Grief-stricken
sum: One evening the lady with the dog sits at the table next to Gurov. Reading her as a respectable, bored newcomer, he is seized by the idea of a quick affair and catches her attention by playing with her dog; she glances at him and looks away.

1| Here, along the walls and around the stove, whole mountains of hospital [junk]{хлама} are [heaped]{навалены}.
